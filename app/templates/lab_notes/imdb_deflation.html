Editor’s note This is a worked example, not a measured study. The film scores and platform averages are illustrative values chosen to show the method; they were not scraped from IMDb, SensCritique or Moviepilot and should not be cited as those platforms’ ratings. Heart of the Beast opened on 25 September 2026, so its real ratings are still moving daily.

In short In five Hollywood films, French and German users score 0.72 points lower than IMDb on average. Rescale each score against the platform it came from and the average gap falls to about 0.05. What remains goes both ways: Europe comes out slightly warmer on Heart of the Beast, slightly cooler on Top Gun: Maverick. The “continental discount” is mostly a difference in scales, not in taste.

The gap

When a mainstream Hollywood film reaches France and Germany, its user rating usually drops. A film scoring 7.2 on IMDb might score 6.8 on Germany’s Moviepilot and 6.7 on France’s SensCritique. The easy reading is that European audiences like these films less.

That reading assumes a 7 means the same thing on every platform. It does not. Each platform has its own average score and its own spread, so a raw comparison mixes two things together: how much people liked the film, and how generously they use the scale.

Why the scales differ

The likeliest cause is school. Nobody scoring a film out of ten starts from a neutral scale; they bring the one they were graded on as children. That is a hypothesis rather than something these numbers can prove, but it points the right way.

US and UK

Percentage grades. Below 70% is close to failing, so a 7 reads as merely acceptable and a 6 as poor.

France

Marks out of 20, where 10 is a pass and 16 is rare. A 6 out of 10 says the film works.

Germany

Grades 1 to 6, where 4 is a pass and top marks are uncommon. High scores are kept for the exceptional.

Three other explanations come up often. French criticism has long prized a director’s personal style over studio polish, which is why some American directors dismissed at home, Michael Mann with Miami Vice or Brian De Palma with Femme Fatale, found champions in Paris. France and Germany also watch most Hollywood films dubbed, and a line re-recorded in a studio months later can flatten a performance. And the audiences differ: IMDb is vast and general and open to organised fan voting, while SensCritique and Moviepilot are smaller communities of keener film fans. None of these is measured here.

Putting the scores on one scale

The fix is to ask a different question of each score: not “how high is it?” but “how far above this platform’s usual score is it?”. Measured that way, a 6.7 on SensCritique and a 7.2 on IMDb are close to the same judgement. Try any film or score below.

About 93% of the raw gap is a difference in how the platforms use the scale. The part that remains is small, changes from film to film, and is where any real difference in taste would have to show up.

Why this matters beyond film

The same trap opens whenever scores from different groups are averaged: satisfaction surveys across countries, expert ratings from different institutions, sentiment scores in different languages. If each group uses the scale differently, the average measures the scales as much as the thing being scored.

It is why every index on this site rescales each input against its own baseline before combining anything. The methodology sets out exactly how these numbers were made, and what a real study would need.

Methodology · {{ note.code }}

How the numbers in this note were made

A two-step rescaling: place each score within its own platform, then translate that position onto any other platform.

The problem

Treat each film as having an underlying quality, and each platform as a lens that maps that quality onto a 1–10 score with its own centre and its own stretch. A raw score difference between two platforms then reflects the lenses as well as the film. The goal is to remove the lens.

Step one: position within the platform

Each score is converted to a z-score, the distance from the platform’s average measured in that platform’s standard deviation:

z = x − μP σP

The z-score is turned into a percentile with the standard normal distribution, which is what the calculator shows.

Step two: translate to another platform

The score on platform B that holds the same position as score x on platform A is:

xB = μB + zA × σB

The “rescaled gap” in the analysis is the average of the German and French z-scores minus the IMDb z-score, multiplied by IMDb’s standard deviation so it can be read in IMDb points next to the raw gap.

Parameters used

These averages and spreads are assumed for the example, not measured from the platforms’ catalogues.

Limits of this example

The bell curve is a simplification. IMDb ratings bunch towards the top of the scale, so a normal distribution misplaces films at the extremes; ranking each film against its platform’s real catalogue would avoid that. Opening-week ratings are also unstable, and Heart of the Beast is days old. Finally, rescaling says how much of a gap is scale. It cannot say whether the remainder comes from taste, dubbing or who uses each site.

What a real study would need

Ratings for the same films on all three platforms, with vote counts and collection dates; platform averages and spreads computed from each full catalogue; films old enough for their ratings to have settled; and a separate test for each explanation of what remains after rescaling. With that data, this note would move from a worked example to a finding.
