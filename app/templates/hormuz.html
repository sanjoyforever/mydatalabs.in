Bottom line

The Strait is {{ exec_summary.level_lower }} and has been for {{ exec_summary.weeks_in_band }} week{{ 's' if exec_summary.weeks_in_band != 1 }}, at {{ "%.1f"|format(exec_summary.points_above_baseline) }} points above calm and {{ "%.1f"|format(exec_summary.off_peak) }} off the peak. The stress is concentrated in {% if exec_summary.top_driver %}{{ exec_summary.top_driver.short }}{% endif %} {%- if exec_summary.drivers|length > 1 %} and {{ exec_summary.drivers[1].short|lower }}{% endif %}, and over the last {{ exec_summary.span_weeks }} weeks the reading has {{ exec_summary.window_direction }} {{ "%+.1f"|format(exec_summary.window_delta) }} points.

None of this forecasts a closure. Five of the seven components are prices or rates, and a price is a market's expectation, not an event: Brent and freight can fall while the shooting continues, and can rise on a rumour that comes to nothing. The two components that count things rather than price them — transits and reroutes — are the ones that say what is physically happening in the Strait, and one of them is published a week in arrears. Read the composite as pressure, not as a prediction.
